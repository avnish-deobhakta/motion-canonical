# DR6: Statistical Analysis Plan (version 1)

**Working title:** Can general-purpose AI models screen for diabetic retinopathy across populations? A preregistered evaluation of frontier and open-weight multimodal models on six public datasets from four countries.

**Status:** registered by commit to `dr6/prereg/SAP_dr6_v1.md` in the public repository, together with the analysis library, the image registry (no grades), the model identifiers and a manifest of hashes that includes the sealed answer key, before any model sees a study image. Changes after registration are logged as dated amendments before unblinding.

**Investigators:** Avnish Deobhakta (PI), Sujai Jaipalli, Jonathan S. Chang, Royce W.S. Chen, Ravi Parikh, Richard B. Rosen, Tak Yee Tania Tai.

## 1. Background and objectives

General-purpose multimodal models are now used to read retinal photographs, but published evaluations of diabetic retinopathy (DR) are single-center, use one dataset, or test models that are no longer current. Our first study showed that frontier models lose most of their accuracy when image quality fails and do not reliably decline to answer. This study asks whether current general-purpose models can perform DR screening at the level required of cleared autonomous systems, whether that performance holds across populations and cameras, and whether a separate decision model can turn a model's free-text description into a better-calibrated grade.

## 2. Hypotheses and comparison hierarchy

**Primary.** For each image model, referable DR sensitivity exceeds 85% and specificity exceeds 82.5% on gradable images. These are the prespecified superiority endpoints of the pivotal trial of an autonomous DR screening system (Abràmoff 2018). A model meets the benchmark only if the lower bounds of both one-sided 97.5% confidence intervals exceed the thresholds (intersection-union test). Five tests (one per model), Holm-adjusted.

**Key secondary.** Structuring a model's free-text findings with a decision model (Jev) changes referable DR balanced accuracy compared with the same model's forced-choice grade on the same images. One two-sided test at alpha 0.05, pooled over the five image models.

**Other secondary (estimates with 95% CIs, no significance tests).**
1. Model versus model, and open-weight versus each closed model.
2. Free text versus forced choice (free text structured by the model itself, by Jev, and by the fixed rule).
3. Free text through Jev versus free text through the self-pass and the fixed rule (the two controls).
4. Performance by dataset and its spread across datasets.
5. Recognition of ungradable images, and false "ungradable" calls on gradable images.
6. Five-level ICDR agreement (quadratic weighted kappa, exact, within one grade) and the share of answers at grade 2.
7. Calibration: stated confidence (forced choice) and Jev probabilities (expected calibration error, Brier score); area under the ROC curve from Jev's probability of referable DR.
8. Repeatability across the three forced-choice repeats.
9. Accuracy by native image resolution (tertile of field-of-view diameter within dataset).
10. Agreement of each text-to-grade mapper with a retina specialist grading the same text blind.

**Exploratory.** Jev reading the image directly. A probe on 16 October 2026 confirmed that the pinned Jev version accepts an image and returns a grade.

## 3. Data

### 3.1 Datasets

| Dataset | Country | Labels used | License or access | Models |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| APTOS 2019 | India | ICDR 0 to 4 (train set) | Kaggle competition rules | All five |
| EyePACS (Kaggle 2015) | United States | ICDR 0 to 4 (train set) | Kaggle competition rules | All five |
| IDRiD | India | ICDR 0 to 4 (grading subset) | CC BY 4.0 | All five |
| DDR | China | ICDR 0 to 4, plus 5 = ungradable | DDR dataset terms | All five |
| SUSTech-SYSU | China | ICDR 0 to 4 (grade 5, laser-treated, excluded) | CC0 | All five |
| BRSET | Brazil | DR_ICDR 0 to 4; quality "Inadequate" = ungradable | PhysioNet Credentialed DUA 1.5.0 | Open-weight model only, on our own compute |

**BRSET.** Under PhysioNet's guidance, sending credentialed data to third-party online services is prohibited unless zero retention, no training use and no human review can be verified. BRSET images are therefore read only by the open-weight model on our own hardware. PhysioNet confirmed before registration that text generated by the open-weight model from BRSET images may be sent to Jev, so BRSET descriptions from the open-weight model are included in the Jev text arm. No BRSET image is sent to Jev or to any other third-party service, so BRSET is excluded from the exploratory Jev image arm.

**Licenses.** Before registration, each dataset's terms are checked for use with commercial model APIs and recorded in `config/licenses.json`. A dataset whose terms prohibit it is moved to open-weight-only, as for BRSET.

### 3.2 Sampling

Fixed seed 20261016. For each dataset, up to 48 gradable images per ICDR grade 0 to 4, drawn at random without replacement, at most one image per patient where a patient identifier exists (EyePACS, BRSET). Where a grade has fewer than 48 eligible images, all are taken (IDRiD grade 1, 25 images, from its training and test sets combined; SUSTech-SYSU grade 1, 24 images). Plus 100 ungradable images from DDR (grade 5) and 100 from BRSET (quality "Inadequate"). The 200 APTOS images used in our first study are excluded.

The sample drawn on 16 October 2026 contains 1,593 images: APTOS 240, EyePACS 240, IDRiD 217, DDR 340 (100 ungradable), SUSTech-SYSU 216 and BRSET 340 (100 ungradable). The 1,153 gradable public images (720 referable, 433 non-referable) form the common set read by all five image models; with the 100 DDR ungradable images, 1,253 images are read by the API models.

### 3.3 Image preparation

Each image is cropped to the fundus field of view, padded to square, resized with Lanczos to 1024 x 1024 pixels and saved as JPEG (quality 95, 4:4:4, no metadata), exactly once. Every model receives identical bytes. Native image size and field-of-view diameter are recorded as covariates, because native resolution tracks grade in some datasets. Images get random identifiers; the key linking identifiers to source files and grades is stored separately and not opened until the scoring freeze is committed.

## 4. Models

**Frontier (API).** One model per vendor (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI), selected by the rule used in our first study: the most capable generally available multimodal API model on the run date, excluding compute-scaled variants, with a preview model used only if no generally available model exists at that tier. Model identifiers were fixed on 16 October 2026 by a probe on three images from our first study (not study images) and are registered with this plan: `claude-fable-5-1` (Anthropic), `gpt-6-astra` (OpenAI), `gemini-4-argon` (Google; generally available since 13 October 2026, so it replaces the Gemini 3.1 Pro preview used in our first study under the same rule) and `grok-4.7` (xAI). Each served model string matched the requested identifier at the probe. Vendor default sampling and reasoning; output ceiling 16,000 tokens. The first served model string per vendor is locked; a change pauses that vendor.

**Open-weight (local).** The most capable open-weight multimodal model that runs at full precision (bf16) on a single 80 GB GPU on the run date; Qwen3.8-27B (`Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B`). The exact Hugging Face revision hash is committed before its first study run. Model generation defaults; run with vLLM on our own hardware (Colab for public datasets; institutional compute for BRSET if required).

**Decision model.** Jev, requested as `typesafe/jev-1.14` via the OpenRouter Decisions API; at the probe it was served as the snapshot `typesafe/jev-1.14-20261014`. The first served snapshot is locked, as for the frontier models; a change pauses the Jev arm and is recorded by amendment.

## 5. Procedures

### 5.1 Stage 1: image to answer

Two prompts, reproduced verbatim in `dr6_lib.PROMPTS`:

- **Forced choice:** the ICDR screening prompt from our first study, with an explicit "ungradable" option. JSON reply with grade, referable and confidence. Three repeats per image.
- **Free text (findings):** describe image quality and every finding with location and extent, then an overall impression, without a numbered grading scale. One repeat per image.

All calls from all vendors and prompts go in one shuffled queue. Expected calls: about 20,050 to the four API models and about 6,370 local runs.

### 5.2 Stage 2: free text to grade

Each stage-1 free-text description (repeat 0) is mapped to an ICDR grade (0 to 4 or ungradable) three ways:

1. **Jev:** a Choice question whose options are the six grades, each with its ICDR definition. Returns a grade and a probability for every grade.
2. **Self-pass (control):** the same model that wrote the description reads it back, text only, and returns a grade.
3. **Fixed rule (control):** a deterministic keyword mapping that applies the ICDR rules to the described lesions (`dr6_lib.rule_grade`), committed with this plan.

Stage-2 inputs contain text only. Stage 2 starts after the stage-1 responses are frozen by hash.

### 5.3 Human text grading (mapper fidelity)

A random sample of 300 descriptions (stratified by source model) is graded on the ICDR scale from the text alone by a retina specialist, blind to source model, dataset, image and reference grade, before the key is opened.

## 6. Outcomes and scoring

- **Referable DR:** reference grade 2 or higher.
- **Index test (primary):** the forced-choice answer. A grade of 2 or more is a positive call. An "ungradable" answer on a gradable image, or an unreadable reply, counts as a referral (positive), as in screening practice. Sensitivity analysis: these calls excluded.
- **Free-text arms:** the mapped grade from each mapper, with the same rules.
- **Gradability:** an "ungradable" answer on an ungradable image is a correct quality call.
- **Parsing:** replies are parsed by `dr6_lib.parse_json_reply` and `norm_grade`; the parsing and mapping code is frozen by hash before the key is opened.

## 7. Statistical analysis

**Weighting.** The sample over-represents higher grades by design. Primary sensitivity and specificity are weighted back to each dataset's own grade distribution (inverse sampling-probability weights within dataset), so that each estimate describes that dataset's full population. Datasets are then weighted equally. Unweighted estimates are reported as secondary.

**Inference.** Each image is an independent eye (one per patient where known). CIs are percentile intervals from 10,000 bootstrap resamples of images, stratified by dataset and grade, seed 20261016. Repeats are averaged within image.

**Primary test.** For each image model, one-sided bootstrap P values for sensitivity above 85% and specificity above 82.5%; the model-level P value is the larger of the two (intersection-union). Holm adjustment across the five models.

**Key secondary test.** Paired difference in referable DR balanced accuracy (mean of weighted sensitivity and specificity), Jev-mapped free text minus forced choice (repeat 0), pooled over image models, image-level paired bootstrap, two-sided.

**Other analyses.** Estimates with 95% CIs, no tests. AUC from Jev probabilities uses P(grade 2, 3 or 4). Calibration uses ten equal-width bins.

**Missing data.** Calls that fail after six attempts are retried in a later session; any still missing are reported and treated as referrals for the primary analysis (sensitivity analysis: excluded). A vendor with a final success rate below 98% is reported but not excluded.

**Software.** Python (NumPy, SciPy, pandas), code hashed and committed with the scoring freeze.

## 8. Sample size

With about 720 referable and 433 non-referable common images, the 95% CI half-width is about 2.6 percentage points for a sensitivity near 85% and about 3.8 points for a specificity near 80%, before the gain from three repeats. This is sufficient to distinguish a model at the benchmark from one 6 to 8 points below it.

## 9. Registration, blinding and freezes

1. This plan, the library (`dr6_lib.py`) and the image registry and hash manifest (no labels) are committed before any study call.
2. Stage-1 responses are frozen by hash before stage 2.
3. Stage-2 responses, human text grades, and parsing and scoring code are frozen by hash before the key is opened.
4. Deviations are logged as dated amendments and reported in a deviations table.

## 10. Reporting

TRIPOD-LLM. Results reported by model, by dataset and pooled, with all prompts, model identifiers, run dates and the deviations table.
